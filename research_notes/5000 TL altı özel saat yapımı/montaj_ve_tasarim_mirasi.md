# Assembly Services, DIY Tooling and Design Heritage for a Custom NH35 Watch in Turkey (research date: 7 Oct 2026)

## 1. What do Turkish watchmakers (saatçi) charge in 2025-2026 for assembly-type services (fitting, dial/hands, regulation, crystal/gaskets, water test, engraving, bracelet sizing)?

### Takeaway
No saatçi in Turkey publishes a price list for "assemble my watch from supplied parts". The closest public data are Armut.com quote requests (most simple mechanical-watch jobs are listed at 250-500 TL, 2025-2026), a few single-shop figures, and brand service lists. Brand lists put a partial service at about 1,300 TL (Tissot) and a full mechanical service at 5,500 TL (Tissot) to 18,800 TL (Longines). For a simple NH35 build, independent-saatçi labour most plausibly lands in the low hundreds to about 2,000 TL. That figure is an inference; no source quotes it.

### Inferences
- The Armut "250 TL" entries recur for very different jobs (from a Panerai that won't run to a Seiko 5 service), so they look like a minimum or placeholder budget the customer entered, not a real quote. They should not be read as market prices.
- Realistic 2026 budget for a saatçi to assemble a customer-supplied NH35 build (case the movement, fit dial and hands, set the date, close the case): about 500-2,000 TL. The lower end is the Armut job levels for simple work. The upper end is the single-shop "movement service 2,000 TL" quote and Tissot's 1,300 TL partial service. Add about 500 TL for caseback engraving and roughly 250-500 TL for bracelet sizing if a shop charges separately. This is an inference; no shop publishes a price for this job.
- Regulation (timing adjustment) of a new NH35 is usually a small job, and some brand services include a free rate check (Longines). Independent saatçis often bundle it with assembly.

### Gaps
- No published saatçi price lists from Kapalıçarşı, Sirkeci, Ankara or İzmir were found online. The researcher's own recommendation is to phone 2-3 shops for written quotes.
- No stand-alone 2026 water-resistance test price was found from a reliable source.
- Technopat and DonanımArşivi threads on "saat kısaltma ücreti" (bracelet shortening fees) exist but returned HTTP 403 and could not be read.

## 3. DIY option: tool-kit prices (Turkey and AliExpress) and difficulty/risk notes for first-time builders

### Takeaway
A workable NH35 tool set costs roughly 2,000-3,500 TL from Turkish retailers: case opener, hand remover, hand-press set, dial protectors and movement holder, with tweezers, blower and finger cots added. A generic AliExpress kit is about $10-30, but since February 2026 it carries about 60% import tax. One set of tools costs about as much as, or more than, one saatçi assembly. DIY pays off only over several builds. The main first-build risks are dust under the crystal, misaligned hands, scratched dials and slipped case openers.

## 4. Design heritage: Takiyüddin, his clock book, "bengâm/binkām", the Istanbul observatory, muvakkithanes and Ottoman clockmakers

### Takeaway
Takiyüddin er-Râsıd (1526-1585) wrote *el-Kevâkibü'd-dürriyye fî vaz'i'l-benkâmâti'd-devriyye* ("The brightest stars for the construction of mechanical clocks"); the colophon dates it 966 H / 1559, Nablus. The word in the title is spelled *benkâmât* in TDV's transliteration (Arabic *binkām*, pl. *binkāmāt*). It originally meant a water clock; he applied it to mechanical clocks. "Bengâm" is a popular Turkish rendering; "bankamat" is not an attested form. His Tophane observatory began work in 1577 and was razed on 22 January 1580.

### Cited Findings
**Takiyüddin (TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi, "Takıyyüddîn er-Râsıd", Hüseyin Gazi Topdemir, vol. 39, 2010):**
- Born 4 Ramazan 932 / 14 June 1526 in Damascus; died 993/1585 in Istanbul. — [TDV: Takıyyüddîn er-Râsıd](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/takiyyuddin-er-rasid)
- TDV gives the clock book's title as *el-Kevâkibü'd-dürriyye fî vażʿi'l-benkâmâti'd-devriyye* and calls it an important work on making mechanical clocks. It gives no year of composition. — [TDV: Takıyyüddîn er-Râsıd](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/takiyyuddin-er-rasid)
- Per TDV (as summarised in my fetch), his clocks included pocket, wall, table and astronomical observation clocks. Their distinguishing feature was showing seconds as well as minutes. — [TDV: Takıyyüddîn er-Râsıd](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/takiyyuddin-er-rasid)
- His other mechanics treatise, *et-Ṭuruḳu's-seniyye fi'l-âlâti'r-rûḥâniyye*, covers clocks, levers, pumps and similar devices. It was edited by Ahmed Yûsuf el-Hasan (Aleppo 1976). — [TDV: Takıyyüddîn er-Râsıd](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/takiyyuddin-er-rasid)
  - TÜBA published a Turkish edition in 2021 and calls it the last and most comprehensive work of the *hiyel* (ingenious devices) tradition. — [TÜBA edition PDF](https://tuba.gov.tr/files/yayinlar/T%C4%B0BKM/TUBA-978-605-2249-96-3.pdf)
  - İhsan Fazlıoğlu says he wrote it at age 26. — [Muslim Heritage, Fazlıoğlu](https://muslimheritage.com/taqi-al-din-works-sci-method/)
- TDV "SAAT" (Atilla Bir and Mustafa Kaçar, vol. 35, 2008) says Takıyyüddin learned clockmaking from foreign clockmakers in Galata ("Saatçiliği Galata'daki yabancı saatçilerden öğrenen Takıyyüddin"). — [TDV: Saat](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/saat)

**Date of the clock book (sources conflict):**
- Muslim Heritage (İhsan Fazlıoğlu, 2008): written in 1559 in Nablus, where he was a judge, per the colophon (966 H). It was "the first time in Islamic and Ottoman world" that mechanical-automatic clocks were treated. It draws on Semiz Ali Paşa's library and his collection of European clocks. Edited by Sevim Tekeli (Ankara 1966). — [Muslim Heritage: Taqi al-Din works](https://muslimheritage.com/taqi-al-din-works-sci-method/)
- An İstiklal column gives 1556 instead, and uses the spelling "El-Kevakibü'd-Dürriye fil-Bengamatü'd-Devriye". — [İstiklal: Osmanlı'da saatçi Dede Ahmed Eflâkî](https://www.istiklal.com.tr/yazarlar/osmanlida-saatci-dede-ahmed-eflaki-952351h)
- The colophon date of 1559 is the better-sourced one.

**The word "bengâm / binkām":**
- TDV transliterates it *benkâm* (pl. *benkâmât*). — [TDV: Saat](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/saat)
- The Qatar Digital Library (British Library manuscripts) glosses Arabic *binkām* as water clock. Its plural *binkāmāt* covers monumental hydraulic machines first used to tell time and later to display astronomical information. Example title: *Kitāb Arshimīdas fī ʿamal al-binkāmāt*. — [QDL: Robots, musicians and monsters](https://www.qdl.qa/robots-musicians-and-monsters-world’s-most-fantastic-clocks); [QDL: Kitāb Arshimīdas fī ʿamal al-binkāmāt](https://www.qdl.qa/en/archive/81055/vdc_100023698323.0x000001)
- Persian *pangān / pang* was a clepsydra: "a copper bason with a small hole in the bottom". — [Etymological Dictionary of Astronomy (Obs. Paris)](https://dictionary.obspm.fr/terms/clepsydras/); [Encyclopaedia Iranica: Clocks](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/clocks-pers)
- I found no source that explicitly derives *binkām* from *pangān*.

**Istanbul observatory (Tophane):**
- After observing from the Galata Tower, he began observations on the Tophane slopes in 985/1577 with Murad III's permission. The observatory and its instruments were destroyed by a hatt-ı hümâyun dated 4 Zilhicce 987 / 22 January 1580. — [TDV: Takıyyüddîn er-Râsıd](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/takiyyuddin-er-rasid)
- He came to Istanbul in 1570 and became chief astronomer (müneccimbaşı) after Mustafa b. Ali el-Muvakkit died in 1571. He used a mechanical clock in his astronomical observations. — [Muslim Heritage, Fazlıoğlu](https://muslimheritage.com/taqi-al-din-works-sci-method/)
  - The same article gives the observatory decision as "early 1579", which conflicts with TDV's 1577 and with the comet-of-1577 imagery on the same page. It is probably an error; follow TDV.
- A Saudi Aramco World article (1977) says his clocks showed hours and minutes and could help fix prayer times without observing the sky. — [Aramco World 1977: Topkapı's Turkish timepieces](https://archive.aramcoworld.com/issue/197704/topkapi.s.turkish.timepieces.htm)

**Muvakkithane and Ottoman timekeepers:**
- TDV "MUVAKKİTHÂNE" (vol. 31): a muvakkit regulated the clocks used to fix prayer times and oversaw their adjustment and repair. Muvakkits were also called "saatçi" and "muvakkit-i salât". The term muvakkithâne was probably first used by the Ottomans. They were usually one or two rooms within a mosque complex. — [TDV: Muvakkithâne (PDF)](https://cdn2.islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/dosya/31/C31010227.pdf)
- The 1470 Fatih Camii muvakkithane is generally considered the first Ottoman muvakkithane. The Bayezid muvakkithane was the most famous; Evliya Çelebi credited its fame to the accuracy of its clocks. — [Belleten article](https://belleten.gov.tr/tam-metin-pdf/215/tur)
- From the late 18th century, large pendulum clocks were placed in muvakkithanes next to major Istanbul mosques. Traditional instruments such as the rubʿu'l-müceyyeb (sine quadrant) stayed in use. — [TDV: Saat](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/saat)
- Timekeeping systems (TDV "Saat"):
  - Alaturka (gurûbî/ezânî) time starts the day at sunset; alafranga (zevâlî) starts at noon or midnight.
  - A 1912 circular made alafranga time mandatory in the army and official offices; Law no. 697 (1925) imposed international time nationally.
  - TDV cites a study on the Meyer family of watchmakers, who made watches showing ezânî time.
  - Source: [TDV: Saat](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/saat)
- Mustafa b. Ali el-Muvakkit, timekeeper of the Sultan Selim mosque, was nicknamed "Koca Saatçi". He became palace chief astrologer in 1560 and died in 1571; his Turkish works became references for Ottoman muvakkits. — [HMML authority record (citing TDV, İhsan Fazlıoğlu)](https://haf.vhmml.org/person/446216782992)

### Inferences
- Name ideas grounded in these sources:
  - "Kevâkib" (stars), from the book's title.
  - "Benkâm", the clock word in Takiyüddin's title. Spell it as TDV does, or "Bengâm" as the popular form; avoid "bankamat".
  - "Râsıd" (observer), Takiyüddin's epithet.
  - "Muvakkit" (timekeeper).
  - "Tophane 1577".
  - "Gurûb" (sunset), from alaturka time.
- A design story can truthfully say that in 1559 an Ottoman scholar wrote a treatise on building mechanical clocks and later used a clock with minute (and per TDV, second) indications at his Istanbul observatory. It should not claim it was "the world's first clock book" without qualification. Muslim Heritage limits the claim to the Islamic/Ottoman world. My fetch summary of TDV "Saat" suggests a broader "bilindiği kadarıyla" (as far as is known) claim, but I could not confirm that wording.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm a dictionary-grade etymology linking *benkâm/binkām* to Persian *pangān*; it is plausible but unsourced.
- TDV's exact wording on second-hand indication was seen only through a summarising fetch, so verify it before quoting.
- Named Ottoman clockmakers beyond Mustafa b. Ali and the Meyer family were not researched in depth (for example Dede Ahmed Eflâkî, who appears only in a newspaper column).

## 5. Design heritage: Seljuk geometric star patterns (8-point stars, 12- and 24-fold rosettes) on Anatolian monuments

### Takeaway
Three well-documented anchors:
- **Konya Karatay Medresesi (1251):** a turquoise and dark-blue tile-mosaic dome covered in rows of **24-pointed stars**.
- **Sivas Gök Medrese (1271):** large **eight-pointed star** panels on the facade, one carrying the Qur'anic verse 68:51, and star motifs on the minaret bases.
- **Divriği Ulu Camii and Darüşşifası (1228-29, UNESCO 1985):** a minbar dated 1243 whose geometric panels secondary sources describe as **12- and 5-pointed stars**.

The eight-armed star is commonly called the "Selçuklu yıldızı" (Seljuk star).

### Cited Findings
- **Karatay Medresesi** (TDV "KARATAY MEDRESESİ", Selçuk Mülâyim, vol. 24, 2001):
  - Built 649/1251 per the portal inscription, by Celâleddin Karatay, under İzzeddin Keykâvus II.
  - The dome's turquoise ("fîrûze") and dark-blue ("lâcivert") tile mosaic, separated by white strips, evokes the sky.
  - "yirmi dört kollu yıldızlar" (24-pointed stars) rise in regular rows from the base to the oculus, linked by knots.
  - The portal has a two-coloured knotted geometric lattice.
  - The dome's oculus and pool led some to call the building an observatory.
  - Source: [TDV: Karatay Medresesi](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/karatay-medresesi)
- The Karatay dome sits on a 24-sided polygon above the pendentives; specialist reference: S. Mülayim (1982), "Konya Karatay Medresesi'nin Ana Kubbe Geometrik Bezemesi". — [Academia.edu: Karatay Medresesi süslemelerinin desen ve tasarım özellikleri](https://www.academia.edu/96376193/Konya_Karatay_Medresesi_s%C3%BCslemelerinin_desen_ve_tasar%C4%B1m_%C3%B6zellikleri)
- **Gök Medrese, Sivas** (TDV "GÖKMEDRESE", Özkan Ertuğrul, vol. 14, 1996):
  - Built 1271 under Gıyâseddin Keyhusrev III by vizier Fahreddin Ali Sâhib Ata; endowment deed Şaban 663 / June 1265.
  - Architect read from the inscription as Kālûyân ("amel-i üstâd Kālûyânü'l-Konevî").
  - Named for its turquoise tiles.
  - The portal has geometric and floral relief, and the minaret bases have "geometrik yıldız motifleri".
  - A panel "sekiz kollu yıldız şeklinde" (eight-pointed star) contains Qur'an 68:51.
  - Source: [TDV: Gökmedrese (Sivas)](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/gokmedrese--sivas)
- **Divriği Ulu Camii and Darüşşifası:**
  - North-portal date 626 H (1228-29). Patron: Mengücekid ruler Ahmadshāh b. Sulaymān; the hospital was founded by Tūrān Malik bint Fakhr al-Dīn Bahramshāh.
  - Architect read as Khurramshāh b. Mughīth al-Khilātī (from Ahlat).
  - UNESCO World Heritage 1985.
  - The north portal is "celebrated for the quality and density of its high-relief stone carving".
  - The wooden minbar is dated 1243 and signed by İbrahim b. Ahmed el-Tiflîsî.
  - Source: [Wikipedia, citing Pancaroğlu 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Mosque_and_Hospital_of_Divri%C4%9Fi)
- Secondary sources on the Divriği minber:
  - The ebony minber's geometric patterns are formed of 12-pointed stars; its stars and panels are 5- and 12-pointed. — [İslam Düşünce Atlası: Divriği](https://islamdusunceatlasi.org/divrigi-ulu-camii-ve-darussifasi/3463); [Sanatın Yolculuğu: Divriği Ulu Camii](https://www.sanatinyolculugu.com/divrigi-ulu-camii/)
  - The dome in front of the mihrab is covered by a 12-segment pyramidal cone, and each bay has a different star vault. — [İslam Düşünce Atlası: Divriği](https://islamdusunceatlasi.org/divrigi-ulu-camii-ve-darussifasi/3463)
- A 12-armed star results from dividing a circle into 12 equal parts (citing Algan 2008). — [Özkul: Divriği motiflerinin tasarım analizi (Academia)](https://www.academia.edu/129126209/K%C4%B0FAYET_%C3%96ZKUL_S%C4%B0VAS_D%C4%B0VR%C4%B0%C4%9E%C4%B0_ULU_CAM%C4%B0_VE_DAR%C3%9C%C5%9E%C5%9E%C4%B0FASI_MOT%C4%B0FLER%C4%B0N%C4%B0N_TASARIM_ANAL%C4%B0Z%C4%B0)
- Anatolian Seljuk star motifs range from 5- to 24-pointed. The eight-pointed star known as the "Selçuklu Yıldızı" holds a particularly important place in Anatolian Seljuk ornament. — [Mersin Üniv. SBE Dergisi: Selçuklu yıldız motifi](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/pub/meusbd/article/1194796)
- Star motifs are defined as figures formed by broken lines intersecting at set points. — [İdil Dergisi: Anadolu Selçuklu sanatı geometrisi](https://idildergisi.com/makale/pdf/1482599248.pdf)

### Inferences
- Watch design fit:
  - A 12-fold rosette maps naturally onto a 12-hour dial; the 12 points can serve as hour indices. This is the Divriği minber geometry, from dividing a circle into 12 equal parts.
  - Karatay's 24-pointed stars on a 24-sided base suit a 24-hour/GMT bezel.
  - The Gök Medrese eight-pointed star suits a caseback or crown motif.
  - The turquoise-and-lacivert palette of the Karatay and Gök Medrese tiles is a well-sourced colour story.
- A combined design story could pair "stars" from Takiyüddin's *el-Kevâkib* (the stars) with the star-filled Karatay dome, which some have even called an observatory. Both are documented facts, but the link between them is a narrative device, not a historical connection.

### Gaps
- The 12-pointed stars on the Divriği minber rest on secondary sources (İslam Düşünce Atlası, Sanatın Yolculuğu, Özkul). The TDV Divriği entry and the UNESCO page could not be read (search page only / HTTP 403).
- Symbolic readings of star counts (for example a lineage reading of the 22 pentagonal stars at Divriği) come from popular writing and were not verified.
